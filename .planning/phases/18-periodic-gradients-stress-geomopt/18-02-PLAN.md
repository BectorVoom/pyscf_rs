<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: `verify_fd` over `Cell` atom coordinates</name>
  Mirror `crates/pyscf-grad/src/verify_fd.rs` exactly — same `FdReport`, same
  `InvalidDisplacement`/`ShapeMismatch` errors, same `oracle_sum` discipline —
  over a `Fn(&Cell) -> Result<f64, PyscfRsError>` energy seam instead of the
  molecular coord closure, because a periodic scanner must carry the lattice
  and the mesh, not just the coordinates.

  **Record the step convention in the doc comment** (18-01 Task 3): this
  harness's `disp` is the HALF step; upstream's is the FULL step; upstream's
  `1e-5` is this harness's `5e-6`.

  **Divide by the realised step, not the nominal one** (D-PBC-31 clause 5).
  `crates/pyscf-grad/src/verify_fd.rs:105-108` forms `plus[ia][c] += disp` and
  divides by `2.0*disp`; the step actually taken is
  `plus[ia][c] - minus[ia][c]`, which differs by up to one ulp of the
  coordinate. **Size it honestly in the doc comment**: at `x ≈ 2` Bohr and
  `disp = 1e-4` that is a `2.2e-12` relative error on the denominator, i.e.
  ~2e-12 Ha/Bohr — two to three orders **below** the ~4e-10 cancellation floor
  18-01 Task 2 measures. It is taken because it is free, not because it moves a
  gate, and no gate is tightened on the strength of it.

  Test with an analytic closure whose gradient is known in closed form (a
  quadratic in the coordinates) — this proves the harness before any real
  gradient exists, which is the whole point of shipping it in wave 0.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: the strain finite-difference harness</name>
  Port `strain_tensor_dispalcement` (`rks_stress.py:59-62`) and
  `_finite_diff_cells` (`:64-84`). Two properties that are easy to get wrong
  and are the reason this is its own task:

  * **Both the lattice AND the atom coordinates are strained** — `:76-77` is
    `cell1 = cell.set_geom_(r.dot(e_strain.T), inplace=False)` followed by
    `cell1.a = a.dot(e_strain.T)`. Straining the lattice alone is a different
    (and wrong) derivative.
  * **The k-points move with the strain, at fixed FRACTIONAL coordinates**
    (D-PBC-31 clause 3). `krks_stress.get_ovlp:88` is
    `scaled_kpts = kpts.dot(cell.lattice_vectors().T)` and `:93-94` re-derives
    `kpts1 = scaled_kpts.dot(cell1.reciprocal_vectors(norm_to=1))` per
    displaced cell. Carrying the original **Cartesian** k-points into the
    displaced cell differentiates a different quantity — correct at Γ, wrong
    everywhere else, and therefore invisible on the obvious fixture. The
    harness returns the transformed k-points alongside the two cells; it does
    not leave the caller to remember.
  * **`cell.space_group_symmetry` triggers a rebuild** (`:82-84`), and
    `check_mesh_symmetry` can ENLARGE `cell.mesh` (`symmetry.py:96`,
    `17-CONTEXT §3`). **Pin the mesh on both displaced cells.** A strain
    difference whose two sides rebuilt to different meshes measures the mesh.

  Test: `_finite_diff_cells(cell, x, y, disp)` returns two cells whose lattice
  vectors differ from the original by exactly `±disp` in the `(x,y)` entry of
  `a·Eᵀ`, whose meshes are equal, and whose volumes bracket the original —
  **and whose returned k-points differ from the original in Cartesian
  coordinates while agreeing in fractional ones**. The last clause is the one
  that fails if clause 3 is missed, and it must fail at `nkpts > 1` on a
  non-cubic cell.
</task>
