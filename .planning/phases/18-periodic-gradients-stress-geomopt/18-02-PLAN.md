<objective>
Turn `crates/pyscf-pbc-grad` from a 13-line stub into the substrate every other
Phase-18 plan builds on: the `Cell`-aware finite-difference harness that is
this phase's primary gate, the strain finite-difference harness the stress half
needs, the neighbour-list-screened `vhf·dm` contraction the gamma half needs,
the tagged density type D-PBC-30 clause 4a needs, and the `aoslice_by_atom`
widening three bodies need.

Nothing here computes a gradient. Everything here is consumed by something that
does, and each piece has a test that does not require any gradient to exist.
</objective>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: the tagged density type (D-PBC-30 clause 4a)</name>
  Upstream tags the density matrix with the orbitals that built it —
  `getattr(dm_kpts, 'mo_coeff', None)` (`fft_jk.py:322`), produced by
  `mf_grad._tag_rdm1(dm0, mo_coeff, mo_occ)` (`grad/rhf.py:50`) — and
  `get_k_e1_kpts` collapses its ket index from `nao` to `nocc` when the tag is
  present (`:357-359`). `18-REVIEW §3.1` sizes that at 755 MiB → 116 MiB on
  diamond `gth-dzvp`.

  A Python attribute on an ndarray has no Rust analogue, so declare the carrier
  explicitly: a density type holding the k-resolved matrices plus
  `Option<(mo_coeff, mo_occ)>`. `KMats` stays exactly as it is — this wraps it,
  it does not replace it, and existing callers are untouched.

  Test: constructing the tag from `mo_coeff`/`mo_occ` and rebuilding the dense
  density from it reproduces `make_rdm1` bit-for-bit on a reference k-point
  SCF solution.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 4: `verify_fd` over `Cell` atom coordinates</name>
  Mirror `crates/pyscf-grad/src/verify_fd.rs` exactly — same `FdReport`, same
  `InvalidDisplacement`/`ShapeMismatch` errors, same `oracle_sum` discipline —
  over a `Fn(&Cell) -> Result<f64, PyscfRsError>` energy seam instead of the
  molecular coord closure, because a periodic scanner must carry the lattice
  and the mesh, not just the coordinates.

  **Record the step convention in the doc comment** (18-01 Task 3): this
  harness's `disp` is the HALF step; upstream's is the FULL step; upstream's
  `1e-5` is this harness's `5e-6`.

  Test with an analytic closure whose gradient is known in closed form (a
  quadratic in the coordinates) — this proves the harness before any real
  gradient exists, which is the whole point of shipping it in wave 0.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 5: the strain finite-difference harness</name>
  Port `strain_tensor_dispalcement` (`rks_stress.py:59-62`) and
  `_finite_diff_cells` (`:64-84`). Two properties that are easy to get wrong
  and are the reason this is its own task:

  * **Both the lattice AND the atom coordinates are strained** — `:76-77` is
    `cell1 = cell.set_geom_(r.dot(e_strain.T), inplace=False)` followed by
    `cell1.a = a.dot(e_strain.T)`. Straining the lattice alone is a different
    (and wrong) derivative.
  * **`cell.space_group_symmetry` triggers a rebuild** (`:82-84`), and
    `check_mesh_symmetry` can ENLARGE `cell.mesh` (`symmetry.py:96`,
    `17-CONTEXT §3`). **Pin the mesh on both displaced cells.** A strain
    difference whose two sides rebuilt to different meshes measures the mesh.

  Test: `_finite_diff_cells(cell, x, y, disp)` returns two cells whose lattice
  vectors differ from the original by exactly `±disp` in the `(x,y)` entry of
  `a·Eᵀ`, whose meshes are equal, and whose volumes bracket the original.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 6: `_contract_vhf_dm` over the existing neighbour list</name>
  Port `pbc/grad/rhf.py:90-131`. It contracts `vhf` against `dm` into a
  `(natm, 3)` array with shell-pair screening from
  `build_neighbor_list_for_shlpairs` — which this port already has
  (`crates/pyscf-pbc-gto/src/neighborlist.rs:221`).

  Both branches ship: screened and unscreened. **The default is decided by
  18-01 Task 4's measurement, not chosen here**, and whichever it is must be
  named in the doc comment rather than inherited from upstream's
  `SCREEN_VHF_DM_CONTRA = True` by transcription (`18-REVIEW §4.1`).

  Accumulation into `de[atom][comp]` goes through `oracle_sum` over a
  materialised per-shell-pair partial buffer, not a running `+=`.

  Tests: (1) screened and unscreened agree to the tolerance 18-01 measured;
  (2) with an artificially huge `rcut` the neighbour list is dense and the two
  are bit-identical; (3) determinism at `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1` and `8` under
  `release-oracle`.
</task>
