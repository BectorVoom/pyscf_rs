**Four substrate pieces that later plans need, none of which is the gradient
crate surface.**

Split out of 18-02 so one session owns one kind of change. 18-02 builds the
`pyscf-pbc-grad` crate and its finite-difference harnesses; this plan widens
types and adds hooks in *other* crates - `pyscf-gto`, `pyscf-pbc-scf`,

**the whole of D-PBC-31 clause 2 rests on**: it is what lets 18-12 and 18-13
replace 36 finite-difference lattice sums with a closed form as a two-line
change rather than new machinery.

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: the tagged density type (D-PBC-30 clause 4a)</name>
  Upstream tags the density matrix with the orbitals that built it —
  `getattr(dm_kpts, 'mo_coeff', None)` (`fft_jk.py:322`), produced by
  `mf_grad._tag_rdm1(dm0, mo_coeff, mo_occ)` (`grad/rhf.py:50`) — and
  `get_k_e1_kpts` collapses its ket index from `nao` to `nocc` when the tag is
  present (`:357-359`). `18-REVIEW §3.1` sizes that at 755 MiB → 116 MiB on
  diamond `gth-dzvp`.

  A Python attribute on an ndarray has no Rust analogue, so declare the carrier
  explicitly: a density type holding the k-resolved matrices plus
  `Option<(mo_coeff, mo_occ)>`. `KMats` stays exactly as it is — this wraps it,
  it does not replace it, and existing callers are untouched.

  Test: constructing the tag from `mo_coeff`/`mo_occ` and rebuilding the dense
  density from it reproduces `make_rdm1` bit-for-bit on a reference k-point
  SCF solution.
</task>

<task type="auto">

  `(natm, 3)` array with shell-pair screening from
  `build_neighbor_list_for_shlpairs` — which this port already has

  Both branches ship: screened and unscreened. **The default is decided by

  `SCREEN_VHF_DM_CONTRA = True` by transcription (`18-REVIEW §4.1`).

  Tests: (1) screened and unscreened agree to the tolerance 18-01 measured;
  (2) with an artificially huge `rcut` the neighbour list is dense and the two
  are bit-identical; (3) determinism at `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=1` and `8` under
  `release-oracle`.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 4: the image-weighted Bloch phase hook (D-PBC-31 clause 2)</name>
  18-12 and 18-13 replace 36 finite-difference lattice sums with a closed form
  (`18-REVIEW §6.2`). The hook that makes it a two-line change there rather
  than new machinery is here.

  `intor_cross_with_images` (`pbc_intor.rs:295`) already builds the Bloch phase
  array `expkl_re/expkl_im` of shape `(nkpts, nimgs)` (`:367-374`) and hands it
  to `lattice_sum` (`:504`). The closed form needs the same lattice sum with
  each image's contribution scaled by a real per-image weight `L_y` — because
  the strain weight `(R_j + L)_y` splits into an **AO-indexed** part `R_{j,y}`
  (applied after the sum, no machinery) and an **image-scalar** part `L_y`
  (which is exactly a phase-array scaling).

  Add an optional `image_weights: Option<&[f64]>` (length `nimgs`) that
  multiplies the phase rows before the sum. **`None` must be bit-identical to
  today** — the existing `crates/pyscf-pbc-gto/tests/pbc_intor.rs` is the proof
  and it runs unchanged.

  Test: with `image_weights = Some(&vec![1.0; nimgs])` the result is
  bit-identical to `None`; with a weight that is `1.0` on image 0 and `0.0`
  elsewhere, the result equals the single-image (`Ls = [[0,0,0]]`) evaluation.
  Two tests, no new integral family, and the whole of clause 2 rests on them.
</task>
