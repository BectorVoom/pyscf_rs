<objective>
**Measure the gate before writing it.** `ROADMAP.md` says Phase 15's gate is
`KMP2 e_corr` to **1e-14**; `PBC-MASTER-PLAN §8.7` plan 15-05 says **1e-8**.
Both are unmeasured guesses, six orders apart, about the same number.

Phase 14 has already paid for this mistake once — `§8.6`'s "1e-15 across every
DF builder" was wrong in both halves and `14-CONTEXT.md` had to replace it with
five measured gates *before* implementation. That is the only reason it did not
cost a rewrite. This plan does the same thing for Phase 15.

**This plan writes no Rust.** It runs upstream against itself, records what the
achievable floor is and what upstream's own internal disagreements are, and
rewrites `15-CONTEXT.md §2` with gates that a correct implementation can pass
and an incorrect one cannot.
</objective>

  number in this plan is measured with the same interpreter.

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: the run-to-run floor of upstream's own e_corr</name>
  The question the gate turns on: **how reproducible is upstream's `e_corr` with
  itself?** Nothing this port does can beat that.

  For each of `diamond` (Bohr-specified, `gth-szv`/`gth-pade`) and `he_fcc`
  (`sto-3g`, all-electron — Phase 14's control cell, where the `exclude_dd_block`
  deferral is provably inert) at k-meshes `[1,1,2]` and `[2,2,2]`:

  * run `KRHF` + `KMP2` **five times in five fresh processes**, at
    `conv_tol = 1e-11` and again at `1e-13`;
  * record the spread of `e_corr` across runs, and the spread across the two
    `conv_tol` values;
  * repeat with `RAYON_NUM_THREADS`/`OMP_NUM_THREADS` at 1 and at 8.

  `.planning/pbc/SUMMARY.md` already records that upstream's printed KRHF energy
  moves by ~1e-15 between runs from its own multi-threaded BLAS. Establish
  whether `e_corr` inherits that, amplifies it, or does something worse. **This
  number is the hard floor of the phase gate.**
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: the DF-route split — upstream against upstream</name>
  `kmp2.py:69` selects between two entirely different `(ia|jb)` routes on
  `isinstance(mp._scf.with_df, df.GDF)`. Measure the gap, on the same cells:

  * `KMP2` over `KRHF(with_df=FFTDF)` — the `fao2mo` route;
  * `KMP2` over `KRHF(with_df=GDF)` — the `_init_mp_df_eris` route;
  * `KMP2` over `KRHF(with_df=MDF)` and `df.RSGDF`, both of which are `GDF`
    subclasses and therefore also take the DF route;
  * additionally, force the FFTDF route on a GDF mean field by setting
    `mymp.with_df_ints = False`, which isolates the **route** from the
    **mean field**.

  Report `|e_corr(FFT) − e_corr(GDF)|` and the four-way table. Phase 14 measured
  the analogous SCF-level gap at **4.5e-6 Ha on diamond** and reproduced it in
  this port to within 1 % — the strongest evidence that phase produced. Find out
  whether the correlation energy shows the same structure.

  **Also vary the FFT mesh** on the FFTDF route (the same ladder Phase 13 used:
  meshes 11/15/21/27) and report whether `e_corr` plateaus, and where. If it
  plateaus at a floor rather than converging monotonically — as the KRHF energy
  did at 2.607e-11, bit-identical at meshes 31 and 41 — say so, because a
  monotone-in-mesh gate would then fail a correct implementation.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 4: the padding path, which no reference cell exercises</name>
  15-CONTEXT §3.4: for every §9.2 system at every mesh run so far, `nocc` is
  k-independent, so `padding_k_idx` is the identity and a padding bug is
  invisible.

  Construct at least one cell where it is NOT — a metal, or a cell where
  `get_nmo(per_kpoint=True)` genuinely varies (upstream's own docstring example
  at `kmp2.py:288-305` is `nmo = (6,6,5)`, `nocc = (2,3,2)`). Candidates in
  order of preference: `si` at a mesh that straddles the gap; an Al or Li cell;
  failing that, a **synthetic** `mo_occ` fed to `KMP2` directly, since
  `get_nocc`/`get_nmo`/`padding_k_idx` read `mp.mo_occ` and nothing else.

  Record, for whichever cell works: `get_nmo(per_kpoint=True)`,
  `get_nocc(per_kpoint=True)`, the dense `nmo`/`nocc`, and the full
  `padding_k_idx(kind="split")` and `(kind="joint")` index lists. **Those lists
  are the tier-2 fixture for plan 15-03 Task 5** and there is no other way to
  get them.

  Also record `frozen` variants on the same cell: `frozen=1`, `frozen=[0,1]`,
  and the per-k-point list form, with the resulting `nocc`/`nmo`/`frozen_mask`.
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 7: the speed floor (D-PBC-28, 15-CONTEXT §7)</name>
  `kmp2.py:69` picks a DF route for correctness reasons (§2.2), but users
  experience it as a performance choice too, and nothing measures which route

  reports its own two routes' ratio against.

  This is a **new** measurement, not a reproduction of an upstream number:
  upstream ships no timing test, only the correctness branch. Say so in the
  README next to the table, the same way 15-CONTEXT §7 says the cost decision
  was never made by anyone until now.

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 7b: price the MO-first transform that plan 15-08 has to build (15-CONTEXT §7.5)</name>
  `fft_ao2mo.general` (`fft_ao2mo.py:102-153`) transforms AO→MO **on the
  real-space grid** (`mos = [lib.dot(c.T, aos[i].T) ...]`, `:147-148`) and hands
  the MO-resolved grid arrays to `_contract_plain`. It never builds the
  `nao² × nao²` AO block. **This port's `fft_general`
  (`crates/pyscf-pbc-df/src/pbc_ao2mo.rs:449-457`) does the opposite** — it calls
  `fft_get_eri` and then a four-index MO transform — so KMP2's non-DF route is
  about to run an `Ng·nao⁴` contraction `nkpts³` times where upstream runs
  `Ng·(nocc·nvir)²`.

  Measure the target, so 15-08 has a number to beat rather than an argument:

  * For `diamond` (`gth-szv`, and again with a larger basis such as `gth-dzvp`)
    and `he_fcc` at `[1,1,2]` and `[2,2,2]`: time upstream's
    `mydf.ao2mo([orbo_i, orbv_a, orbo_j, orbv_b], kpts_quadruple)` — the exact
    call `kmp2.py:99-103` makes — and separately time upstream's
    `mydf.get_eri(kpts_quadruple)` (the AO block this port materialises).
    Record wall clock, and `nao`, `nocc`, `nvir`, `ngrids` alongside, so the
    ratio can be checked against §7.0's `(nao²/(nocc·nvir))²` prediction.
  * Record peak RSS for both, via `resource.getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF).ru_maxrss`
    around each call in a fresh process. `nao⁴` complex is 1.6 GB at
    `nao = 100`, and this machine already exit-137s on 17-12's host suite.
  * State plainly in the README whether the predicted ratio held. If the
    measured gap is far smaller than `(nao²/(nocc·nvir))²` at the reference
    sizes, **say so** — 15-08 is then a scaling fix rather than a
    reference-system fix, and its gate should be stated at the larger basis.
</task>
