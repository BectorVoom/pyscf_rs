The space group and the AO-rotation machinery: `SPGElement`/`SpaceGroup`
(`space_group.py`, 369 l) and `Symmetry` + Wigner-D matrices + the three
one-quantity transforms (`symmetry.py`, 348 l). Then wire the result into

produces block-diagonal AO rotations, `mo_coeff` is COLUMN-MAJOR
(`pyscf-pbc-scf/src/types.rs:119`), and a row/column transposition here is
exactly the shape of 14-05's `decompose_j2c` misread — **worth +6 306 866.73 Ha
and invisible to every gate then existing.**

<task type="tdd">
  <name>Task 1: space_group.rs — SPGElement</name>
  `space_group.py:30-248`. A rotation plus a fractional translation, with basis
  conversions (`a2b`, `a2r`, `b2a`, `b2r`, `r2a`, `r2b`, `:207-241`), the group
  ops (`dot`, `dot_rot`, `inv`, `transform`), the predicates (`rot_is_eye`,
  `rot_is_inversion`, `trans_is_zero`, `is_eye`, `is_inversion`) and a total
  order (`__lt__` … `__ge__`, `:177-195`) plus `__hash__` (`:197`).

  `transform_rot` (`:30`) carries an `allow_non_integer` flag; when `False` a
  non-integer result is an error, and that is how a wrong basis conversion is
  caught. Keep the flag — do not "simplify" it to always-allow.

  The ordering and hash matter for the same reason as 17-02 Task 4: they fix the
  order operations are enumerated in, which `stars_ops` exposes.

  Tests: the six basis conversions round-trip to 1e-13 on `si` and `graphene`;
  the diamond glide is `trans_is_zero() == false` while every `lif` op is
  `true` — that single assertion is what distinguishes symmorphic from not.
</task>

<task type="tdd">
  <name>Task 3: symmetry.rs — the Wigner-D matrices</name>
  `symmetry.py:32-94`. `get_Dmat(op, l)` builds the real-spherical-harmonic
  rotation for angular momentum `l`; `get_Dmat_cart` the Cartesian one;
  `make_Dmats(cell, ops, l_max)` assembles them per op.

  **This is the §3.2 trap.** Give the AO rotation exactly ONE implementation and
  pin it with the identity that *defines* it, not with a round-trip:

  ```
  for every op:  R(op) · S · R(op)ᴴ == S      (S = pbc_intor int1e_ovlp at Γ)
  ```

  where `R` is assembled through `make_rot_loc` (`:330-343`) over the shell
  layout. That test needs no oracle, catches a transposed block, a wrong
  `make_rot_loc` offset and a wrong `l` convention, and it is the analogue of
  14-05's `V j2c Vᴴ = I` regression — the test that was missing when the
  6 306 866 Ha defect shipped.

  Second oracle-free test: `R(op₁) R(op₂) == R(op₁ ∘ op₂)` over the whole group.
  A representation that is not a homomorphism is not a representation.

  Third: `is_eye(op)` ⟹ `R == I` to 1e-14.
</task>

<task type="tdd">
  <name>Task 4: symmetry.rs — check_mesh_symmetry, and Cell's mesh can GROW</name>
  `symmetry.py:96-131`, consumed by `Cell.symmetrize_mesh` (`cell.py:1529-1550`)
  and `Cell.build_lattice_symmetry` (`:1552-1580`).

  Port all three. `symmetrize_mesh` **enlarges** the FFT mesh when the
  user's mesh does not carry the lattice symmetry, and upstream emits a loud
  warning when it grows more than 8× *and* by more than 1000 points
  (`cell.py:1541-1549`). Port the warning verbatim — it is the user's only
  signal that their `mesh` was changed underneath them.

  **Document at every call site** that this is why 17-CONTEXT §3.3 forces every
  two-run gate to pin the mesh on both sides. Add the note to `Cell::build`'s
  doc comment too: turning `space_group_symmetry` on can change `cell.mesh` and
  therefore the energy, for reasons unrelated to the IBZ.

  `build_lattice_symmetry`'s `check_mesh_symmetry` flag selects between "lower
  the group to fit the mesh" (`true`, the default) and "raise the mesh to fit
  the group" (`false`). Both branches ship; the flag is not a nicety, it is what
  makes a non-symmorphic cell usable at a user-chosen mesh.
</task>

<task type="tdd">
  <name>Task 5: symmetry.rs — Symmetry, and the reference-cycle non-port</name>
  `symmetry.py:132-224`. `Symmetry::build(space_group_symmetry, symmorphic,
  check_mesh_symmetry)` fills `spacegroup`, `ops`, `Dmats`, `l_max`,
  `has_inversion`, `nop`.

  **17-CONTEXT §3.9:** upstream deletes `lattice_symmetry.cell` and
  `lattice_symmetry.spacegroup.cell` afterwards (`cell.py:1576-1579`) purely to
  break a Python refcount cycle. In Rust that cycle does not exist, so the
  deletion has no meaning — **but do not let `Symmetry` own a `Cell` either.**
  Build from a borrowed `&Cell`; store only lattice-derived data. A cloned
  `Cell` would silently desynchronise on the next `cell.build()`, which is a
  worse bug than the one upstream was avoiding. Say all of this in the doc
  comment so the "missing" deletion is obviously deliberate.
</task>
